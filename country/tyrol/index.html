Tyrol Mountain Passes: Frequently Asked Questions

Are Tyrol mountain pass driving conditions updated in real-time? ▼
Yes. LiveRoadStatus updates road surface status, winter traction laws, and avalanche closures for all 20 Tyrolean mountain passes every 5 minutes using official feeds from ÖAMTC (Österreichischer Automobil-, Motorrad- und Touring Club) and ASFINAG.
What are the legal winter tire and snow chain mandates in Austria? ▼
In Austria, situational winter equipment is legally mandatory between November 1 and April 15 whenever roads are covered with snow, slush, or ice. Winter tires must have M+S or 3PMSF markings with a minimum tread depth of 4 mm for radials. Snow chains must be fitted on driven wheels when indicated by official road signs.
Is Arlberg Pass (B197) open when the Arlberg Expressway Tunnel (S16) is closed? ▼
The B197 Arlberg Pass road (1,793 m) acts as the official toll-free detour when the S16 Arlberg Tunnel undergoes maintenance. However, during severe alpine winter storms or high avalanche danger levels, snow chain requirements or temporary pass closures may be enacted on the pass road.
When is Timmelsjoch High Alpine Road open? ▼
Timmelsjoch (2,474 m / 8,117 ft) connects Ötztal with South Tyrol (Italy). Because of heavy high-elevation snowfall, it operates as a seasonal toll road, closing in late October / early November and reopening after extensive snowblower clearance in late May or early June.
Are there motorcycle noise restrictions on Hahntennjoch? ▼
Yes. To protect local alpine communities, Land Tirol enforces a standing noise limit of 95 dB (stationary noise / Nahfeld-Standgeräusch) for motorcycles on L246 Hahntennjochstraße during summer months (May 15 to October 31).
