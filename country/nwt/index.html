Northwest Territories Passes & Ice Roads: Frequently Asked Questions

When do NWT winter ice roads open and close? ▼
Winter ice roads in the Northwest Territories—including the Mackenzie Valley Winter Highway (Norman Wells Pass corridor) and the Tibbitt to Contwoyto Ice Road—typically open between mid-January and early February once ice thickness reaches 70–100 cm. They close annually in late March or early April due to spring thaw. NWT Infrastructure 511 provides daily updates on weight restrictions and ice profiles.
Can you drive a vehicle across Canol Road Mountain Crossings in NWT? ▼
No, the NWT portion of the Canol Road past Macmillan Pass is an unmaintained wilderness trail known as the Canol Heritage Trail. All WWII bridges have collapsed or washed out, and river crossings like the Twitya River require packrafts or cable cars. It is exclusively an extreme multi-week wilderness expedition route for experienced hikers.
Is the Inuvik-Tuktoyaktuk Highway (Hwy 13) open year-round to the Arctic Ocean? ▼
Yes! Completed in 2017, the Inuvik-Tuktoyaktuk Highway (Highway 13) is Canada's only all-weather highway extending to the Arctic Ocean. Drivers travel across 138 km of permafrost tundra hills. Severe arctic blizzards and whiteouts can cause temporary winter closures.
Where can I check live NWT highway advisories? ▼
LiveRoadStatus monitors official updates directly from the Government of Northwest Territories Department of Infrastructure (DOT.gov.nt.ca) and Parks Canada bulletins.
