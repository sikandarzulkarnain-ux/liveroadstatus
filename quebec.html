Quebec Mountain Passes: Frequently Asked Questions

What are Quebec mandatory winter tire laws under the Highway Safety Code? ▼
In Quebec, winter tires bearing the 3-Peak Mountain Snowflake (3PMSF) symbol are legally mandatory on all passenger vehicles registered in Quebec from December 1 to March 15 under the Highway Safety Code. On mountain pass routes like Route 389 (Col Gagnon), Route 299 (Chic-Chocs), and Route 381 (Grands-Jardins), high-elevation snowfall can persist into late spring.
What are travel advisories for Route 389 to Fermont (Gagnon Pass)? ▼
Route 389 spans 570 km between Baie-Comeau and Fermont across remote boreal forest. Crossing Col Gagnon (620 m), drivers face unpaved gravel segments, active heavy mining trucks, long distances without fuel stations, and extreme winter ground blizzards.
What permits and bear safety measures are required for Torngat Mountains hikes? ▼
Trekking in Torngat Mountains National Park Reserve on the Quebec / Labrador border requires mandatory registration with Parks Canada and an orientation at Torngat Mountains Base Camp. Certified Inuit bear guards or satellite tracking beacons are required due to dense polar bear and black bear populations.
Where can I check live Québec 511 highway conditions? ▼
LiveRoadStatus updates live road conditions directly from Québec 511 (quebec511.info) managed by the Ministère des Transports et de la Mobilité durable (MTMD).
