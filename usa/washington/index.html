Washington Winter Vehicle Chain Law Regulations (WSDOT)

During severe winter weather, Washington State Department of Transportation (WSDOT) and Washington State Patrol strictly enforce chain requirements on all mountain pass routes.

Traction Tires / Chains Required

All passenger vehicles must have approved traction tires (M+S or 3-Peak Mountain Snowflake) or carry tire chains when "Traction Tires Required" is declared on Snoqualmie Pass (I-90), Stevens Pass (US-2), or Blewett Pass (US-97).

Chains Required on All Vehicles

When "Chains Required" is posted, tire chains must be installed on drive wheels of all vehicles, including 4WD and AWD vehicles (unless AWD with snow tires and carrying chains). Penalty for bypassing chain checkpoints is a $500 state fine.

Frequently Asked Questions — Washington Mountain Passes

Answers to common traveler questions regarding Washington pass road conditions, avalanche closures, and WSDOT updates.

The highest year-round maintained highway pass in Washington is Sherman Pass on SR-20 in the Kettle River Range at 5,575 feet (1,699 m). The highest seasonal pass is Washington Pass on the North Cascades Highway (SR-20) at 5,477 feet.
Snoqualmie Pass on I-90 is WSDOT’s highest priority winter corridor and remains open almost continuously. Temporary rolling closures occur only during active avalanche control blasting or vehicle spin-out clearance.
The North Cascades Highway (SR-20 over Washington Pass) typically closes in mid-November following major snow accumulation across 70+ avalanche chutes and reopens in early to mid-May after WSDOT clearing crews work for 4-6 weeks.
