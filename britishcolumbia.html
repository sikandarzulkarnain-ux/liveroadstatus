British Columbia Mountain Passes: Frequently Asked Questions

What are BC winter tire and commercial vehicle chain law requirements? ▼
In British Columbia, winter tires are legally required on designated high-elevation highways—including Rogers Pass, Coquihalla Highway (Hwy 5), Kootenay Pass (Hwy 3), Allison Pass, and Pennask Summit (Hwy 97C)—from October 1 to April 30. Tires must carry the M+S (Mud & Snow) or 3PMSF (3-Peak Mountain Snowflake) symbol with a minimum tread depth of 3.5 mm. Commercial trucks over 11,794 kg GVWR must carry tire chains.
How often are Rogers Pass and Kootenay Pass avalanche control closures updated? ▼
Rogers Pass (Glacier National Park) and Kootenay Pass (Highway 3 Salmo-Creston) undergo active winter avalanche control operations involving explosives and artillery shelling conducted by Parks Canada and Canadian Armed Forces. Road closures are updated live on DriveBC and typically range from 1 to 4 hours per operation.
What is the highest paved highway pass in British Columbia? ▼
Kootenay Pass on Highway 3 (Salmo-Creston Highway) is the highest paved mountain pass in British Columbia at 1,775 meters (5,823 feet). Pennask Summit on Highway 97C (Okanagan Connector) is the second highest paved summit in BC at 1,728 meters (5,669 feet).
What permits are required for Chilkoot Pass and Berg Lake / Robson Pass alpine treks? ▼
The Chilkoot Pass / Chilkoot Trail requires a seasonal backcountry hiking permit issued jointly by Parks Canada and the US National Park Service. Robson Pass via the Berg Lake Trail in Mount Robson Provincial Park requires advance trail reservations through the BC Parks Day-Use / Backcountry Reservation System.
Where can I view live DriveBC highway webcams? ▼
LiveRoadStatus links directly to official Ministry of Transportation & Infrastructure (MoTI) DriveBC live cameras for Rogers Pass, Coquihalla, Kootenay Pass, Allison Pass, Pine Pass, and Alaska Highway summits.
