New Brunswick & Nova Scotia Passes: Frequently Asked Questions

What are Suête wind warnings on Cape Breton mountain passes? ▼
Suête winds are intense, localized southeasterly downslope windstorms in western Cape Breton, Nova Scotia. Blowing off the Cape Breton Highlands plateau down towards Cheticamp, winds can top 140–160 km/h, causing temporary highway closures for high-profile vehicles on Cheticamp Pass and North Mountain.
Are winter tires mandatory on Cabot Trail and Mount Carleton passes? ▼
Neither Nova Scotia nor New Brunswick mandates specific calendar-based winter tire laws like Quebec, but winter tires (3PMSF rated) are essential from November to April due to extreme elevation snowpack, sudden ice squalls, and steep 13% grades on North Mountain and Restigouche Hwy 180.
What is the highest road point in the Canadian Maritime provinces? ▼
The highest paved mountain road passes in the Maritimes occur on the Cabot Trail Plateau in Nova Scotia (460 m / 1,509 ft) and on Route 180 / Mount Carleton Provincial Park roads in New Brunswick (~450 m / 1,476 ft).
Where can I check live 511 highway conditions for NB and NS? ▼
LiveRoadStatus monitors real-time driving conditions directly from 511 NB (511.gnb.ca) and 511 NS (511.novascotia.ca), alongside Parks Canada alert bulletins for Cape Breton Highlands National Park.
